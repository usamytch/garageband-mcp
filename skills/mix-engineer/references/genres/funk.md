# Funk / disco-funk / heavier funk hybrids

**Aim:** a clear rhythmic conversation between bass, drums and short guitar/keyboard gestures. First decide whether the reference is dry live funk, polished disco-funk or an aggressive rock-funk hybrid. Preserve the pocket and intentional dynamic accents.

## Engineer evidence

Mick Guzauski describes *Random Access Memories* as an acoustic/electronic blend designed to feel organic. Tone came largely from arrangement and recording; the mix did not require extreme correction. He blended different kick-mic functions and used compression on bass, vocals, guitars and vocoder vocals. This does not imply that weak home recordings need no repair. [Universal Audio interview](https://www.uaudio.com/blogs/ua/artist-interview-mick-guzauski), source `guzauski-ua-2013`.

Manny Marroquin describes bringing more low-end weight to John Mayer's *Gravity* and making mix decisions from the lyrics; this supports a musical priority, not an all-funk EQ setting. [Waves interview, 2021](https://www.waves.com/waves-grammys-2021-manny-marroquin-post-malone), source `marroquin-2021`.

## Multitrack decisions — our proposed workflow

1. Identify the pulse anchor and its answering parts. Annotate a representative groove, a fill, a sparse verse and a busy chorus. Note which pushes/pulls are repeated musical intention before considering timing edits.
2. Build kick, snare, hats and bass into a coherent groove with minimal processing. Bass is a melodic/rhythmic lead in many arrangements; set it with the actual ghost notes and sustained notes present, not a single loud slap.
3. Check multiple bass or kick sources for polarity, timing and redundancy. Separate same-source alignment from a musician's pocket: aligning a DI to its amp is different from moving the bassist onto the kick grid.
4. Diagnose bass unevenness by note, articulation and register. A slap transient, a long low note and an intentionally muted ghost note are different problems. Compare gentle broad dynamics control, a local level edit or selective tonal correction. Judge attack and recovery with the groove; do not make all notes equal.
5. Add guitar chops, clavinet, keys and percussion one at a time. Assign each an audible rhythmic slot. If two parts obscure each other's attack, test level/pan and arrangement role before high-pass filtering both into thinness. Avoid making the sixteenth-note surface brighter with each added part.
6. Use ambience to imply a common space while preserving gaps between notes. A dry rhythm section with a longer vocal texture is a valid contrast. Test decay against the next relevant event; there is no rule that every effect must be a tempo subdivision.
7. Add vocal and hook detail, then rebalance the groove. Check that backing vocals or a vocoder chord do not remove bass pitch or percussion motion. For heavy funk, preserve rhythmic separation while borrowing guitar weight from the metal/rock profile, rather than importing their entire drum envelope.

| Symptom | First discriminating test | Reject the change when |
|---|---|---|
| Bass is loud but groove is vague | Compare note tails and kick overlap at matched level | More compression hides articulation |
| Guitar chops are sharp but rhythm is flat | Bypass dynamics and compare accent hierarchy | Every muted stroke becomes equally prominent |
| Disco sheen becomes tiring | Compare hats, guitar and vocal brightness together | Individual solo improvements accumulate into glare |
| Heavy chorus stops dancing | Reduce rhythm-guitar density temporarily | Added sustain fills every intentional gap |

**Vocal tuning and character:** preserve expressive blue notes, scoops and vibrato unless the brief calls for an electronic lead. A vocoder uses a carrier shaped by speech; it is not a synonym for pitch correction or distortion. Plan carrier notes/chords with the song's harmony and rhythm, keep intelligibility deliberate, and test a dry-vocal blend as an artistic alternative.

**GarageBand execution:** keep groove diagnosis distinct from editing. Use supported balance and controls first. Never replace a missing transient shaper, sidechain or parallel return with an unlabelled macro approximation. Check [adapter capabilities](../garageband.md) before choosing the execution path.

**Acceptance:** the bass line remains singable; the pocket feels stable without becoming mechanical; ghost notes retain hierarchy; the hook and backbeat survive quiet playback and mono; the track stays engaging through a full section at matched loudness. Numbers alone cannot establish groove.
