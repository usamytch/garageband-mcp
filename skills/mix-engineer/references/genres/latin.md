# Latin: identify the tradition and production first

**Coverage:** Latin pop and reggaeton/urban pop are directly supported by the researched engineer material. For salsa, bachata, samba, flamenco or another tradition, retain that tradition's rhythmic roles and seek a relevant reference; do not present an urban-pop chain as universal Latin expertise. A rock/funk arrangement with Latin percussion is a hybrid, not automatically reggaeton.

## Engineer evidence

Josh Gudwin's *Despacito* remix used Jaycen Joshua's already mixed stems. He changed balances and vocal arrangement, preserved the underlying mix, and integrated separately comped/tuned vocals and effects. This is evidence for respecting finished stems; it is not a raw-multitrack recipe or a disclosure of Joshua's original mix settings. [AudioTechnology interview, 2017](https://www.audiotechnology.com/features/mix-masters-biebers-despacito), source `gudwin-despacito-2017`.

Gudwin's later interview discusses section-by-section work with Tainy and production sounds whose effects define their identity. It distinguishes a designed synth/reverb sound from a vocal that benefits from separately controllable effects. ## Multitrack decisions — our proposed workflow

1. Identify the rhythmic grammar actually present. Label pulse, backbeat, subdivisions, fills and call-and-response parts from the recording/reference. Note tempo or groove changes; a single static pumping/decay treatment may fail across sections.
2. Determine whether the input is raw tracks, a produced session or mixed stems. Do not dismantle working production effects to create an artificial clean starting point. If a stem combines drums and percussion, acknowledge that their internal balance cannot be independently edited.
3. Build bass and the main rhythmic pattern with lead vocal present. Separate low-end weight from the identity of the offbeat/snare/percussion pattern. Check note duration and competing attacks before making the kick and bass louder.
4. Bring in percussion by function. Make important syncopations readable while secondary texture remains secondary. Check stereo placement against the reference and mono; avoid scattering every percussion track to an extreme. For acoustic instruments, preserve body and useful room rather than leaving only bright clicks.
5. Place guitars/keys/brass or synth hooks around the vocal. Use phrases and gaps to decide what needs prominence. In a live Latin-rock blend, prioritize the drum/percussion relationship and coherent ensemble perspective; do not add a synthetic low-end aesthetic merely because the language is Spanish.
6. Treat lead, rap sections, doubles, harmonies and ad-libs as different roles. Ride word endings and fast phrases where needed. Verify lyric meaning before creating throws or repeated syllables; do not derive linguistic decisions from amplitude segmentation.
7. Design transitions in context: a percussion pickup, bass dropout, vocal texture change or controlled echo can matter more than broad master processing. Arrangement changes require the user's intended scope; mixing authorization is not blanket permission to rewrite the song.

| Symptom | First discriminating test | Reject the change when |
|---|---|---|
| Low end is strong, groove is weak | Compare bass tails and key syncopated details | Sub boost further hides the rhythmic identity |
| Percussion is detailed but tiring | Listen to the complete high-frequency layer | Each isolated brightness boost accumulates into glare |
| Vocal feels buried after compression | Compare the printed vocal ambience | Compression raises room/tails above the next phrase |
| Acoustic colour disappears | Compare body and natural transient before filtering | Parts become interchangeable clicks |
| A new section feels like another song | Compare its anchor balance and retained production effects | A uniform preset destroys the intended transition |

**Vocal tuning and effects:** preserve melodic ornament and local harmonic exceptions. A tuned lead, rap texture and vocoder harmony are different jobs. For electronic layers, determine carrier notes and rhythmic articulation explicitly; keep a dry or lightly processed alternative for comparison. Do not infer key from genre or language.

**GarageBand execution:** use verified balance/pan and exposed processing first. A lack of independent sends or external sidechain routing must remain visible in the plan. Use the current [adapter contract](../garageband.md) to decide between native controls, an identified offline render or a bounded manual step.

**Acceptance:** the groove remains recognisable; lead articulation and essential percussion survive quiet playback; bass pitch and weight translate; acoustic timbres retain their identity; section changes remain intentional; mono preserves the main pattern. This profile offers a strong urban-pop starting workflow, not validated mastery of every Latin tradition.
