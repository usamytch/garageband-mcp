# Metal

**Aim:** intelligible riffs and low-end articulation at high arrangement density. Specify subgenre: a sparse doom arrangement and fast extreme metal need different envelopes and priorities.

**Working hypotheses:** determine whether kick, bass or guitar supplies each low-frequency role. Compare palm-mute clarity before/after any low-mid reduction; do not remove the instrument's weight merely to tidy a spectrum. Evaluate consonants and cymbal fatigue with all guitars playing. Consider source/performance conflicts before processing them harder.

**Compare:** note separation in the busiest passage, low-end continuity, vocal intelligibility, mono behavior and sustained listening fatigue. Preserve section contrast rather than making quieter passages equally dense.

**Avoid:** automatic sample replacement, universal high-pass frequencies, or extreme-metal settings applied to every metal subgenre.

**Source observation:** Mark Mynett's engineer-authored [Mixing Metal](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/mixing-metal) (Sound On Sound, 2009) addresses definition and tightness in extreme metal. Accessible indexed excerpts support that scope; this profile's broader decision prompts are proposed experiments. Provenance: `mynett-2009` in [sources.json](../sources.json).
