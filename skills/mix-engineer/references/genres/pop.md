# Pop: lead, hook and section contrast

**Aim:** an unmistakable emotional focus, coherent low end and a hook that survives everyday playback. Choose the relevant production world: intimate/acoustic, band-driven, dance-pop or deliberately synthetic. An artist's characteristic effect may be part of the composition.

## Engineer evidence

Serban Ghenea explicitly rejects a fixed bag of tricks: identify what moves the listener in this song and maximize it. His public interview does not disclose a reproducible secret vocal chain. [Waves interview, 2021](https://www.waves.com/waves-grammys-2021-serban-ghenea-taylor-swift), source `ghenea-2021`.

Tony Maserati describes selective subtractive EQ, riding a heavily compressed parallel vocal for proximity, and changing delay/ambience with the section and phrasing. This illustrates distinct jobs for tone, density and depth; it does not make parallel compression mandatory. [Audiofanzine interview, 2018](https://en.audiofanzine.com/sound-technique/editorial/articles/mixing-in-the-fast-lane.html), source `maserati-2018`.

Josh Gudwin asks for production sounds to retain their character and, where useful, separately printed dry audio and effects. His work with Dua Lipa and Latin artists emphasizes building on the accepted production. [Mix interview, 2021](https://www.mixonline.com/recording/josh-gudwin-on-mixing-part-1-juanes-bad-bunny-and-dua-lipa), source `gudwin-2021`.

## Multitrack decisions — our proposed workflow

1. Locate the lead, hook, pulse, bass and section transitions. Separate alternate takes from intended doubles; note tuning, pumping, distortion and ambience already printed into each source. Preserve a rough mix as the production-intent reference.
2. Establish the lead and hook in the busiest section, then find a useful relationship with kick/bass and supporting harmony. Do not make every layer equally audible. The mix should guide attention without a listener needing a track list.
3. Prepare the vocal as an expressive performance: valid comp, clicks/breaths, needed pitch repair, local level control, tone, dynamics, sibilance and space. This is a decision order, not a rigid plugin order. Recheck de-essing after brightness or saturation changes, and check breaths after compression.
4. Identify the real low-end conflict: simultaneous transients, overlong sustain, notes with unequal energy or competing fundamentals. Test one cause at a time. Sidechain pumping is useful only if it fits the groove and is actually available; it is not the default cure.
5. Distinguish lead doubles, harmonies, ad-libs and textures. Lead consonants anchor intelligibility; competing consonants may need local attention while vowel width remains useful. Do not quantize all doubles so tightly that their character disappears.
6. Use depth intentionally. Compare a dry/intimate verse with a larger chorus, or another reference-led contrast. Delay throws should answer a musical/lyrical phrase, not repeat arbitrary detected syllables. A long tail must not blur the next line unless that blur is desired.
7. Evaluate the chorus arrival, bridge retreat and final hook as transitions. A fader, supporting layer or selective effect can create motion without driving the entire mix harder into a limiter.

| Symptom | First discriminating test | Reject the change when |
|---|---|---|
| Vocal is polished but detached | Compare depth/ambience and accompaniment balance | More compression removes natural phrasing |
| Lead sounds lispy | Compare sibilants before/after de-essing in context | Brightness is replaced by missing consonants |
| Bass vanishes on small playback | Compare harmonic audibility, not only sub level | Saturation obscures pitch or roughens the wrong section |
| Chorus does not lift | Compare preceding section and hook hierarchy | Extra master loudness is the only audible difference |
| Hook has impressive width but weak centre | Listen to mono and one-channel playback | Essential melody depends on a cancellation-prone effect |

**Vocal tuning:** establish key, local chords and intended notes; chromatic approach notes may be right. Natural repair preserves transitions/vibrato, while deliberately fast tuning creates a separate sound. Compare formants and timing after any pitch shift. Never infer a complete melody from key alone. Use a vocoder for a designed synth/speech layer, not as a fix for inaccurate singing.

**GarageBand execution:** distinguish an exposed native correction control from note-by-note editing, and a Smart Control macro from a compressor parameter in physical units. Confirm available processing/routing before proposing a chain. Offline effects should be rendered to separate identified files and compared after re-import. See [adapter contract](../garageband.md).

**Acceptance:** quiet-playback hook recognition; words and breath naturalness; stable low-end pitch; expressive section contrast; mono compatibility; no new tuning warble or consonant damage. Delivery loudness is a separate choice from mix quality, not a fixed genre number.
