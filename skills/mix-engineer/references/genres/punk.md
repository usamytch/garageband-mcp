# Punk / pop-punk

**Aim:** direct impact, a readable vocal and deliberate guitar density. Choose raw/live punk, melodic pop-punk or a heavier hybrid from the brief and reference. Preserve intentional raggedness; the cleanest solo is not necessarily the right mix.

## Engineer evidence

Tom Lord-Alge describes upfront drums, making competing guitar parts audible through EQ, and adding a distorted centre guitar especially in choruses. He also checks the meaning of words before making vocal repeats in an unfamiliar language. These are conditional decisions from his work, not a compulsory chain. [Waves interview, 2017](https://www.waves.com/inside-the-mix-tom-lord-alge), source `tla-2017`.

Andy Wallace's fast full-band balance and later detailed rides provide a useful complementary approach: solve the relationship between parts before perfecting isolated sounds. [Linkin Park interview, 2014](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-linkin-parks-hunting-party), source `wallace-2014`. See [engineer methods](../engineer-methods.md) for the attribution boundaries.

## Multitrack decisions — our proposed workflow

1. Map the chorus, verse, stops, fills and final refrain. Identify actual double takes, alternate guitar mics, vocal doubles and gang vocals. Do not mistake an amp mic for another performance or enable an unused take.
2. Build a balance in the busiest refrain with lead vocal present. Establish snare/backbeat against guitar attack, then the kick–bass relationship. Return to the verse before committing: a drum sound that works under four guitars can dominate a sparse section.
3. For live drums, audition close mics together with overheads/room. Investigate same-source cancellation before brightening the snare. Clean only unwanted events, retain fill tails and deliberate bleed. Sample reinforcement is a separate production decision.
4. Use bass to connect guitar harmony and drum pulse. If pitch disappears on small playback, compare a midrange or restrained saturation change before boosting sub-bass. If kick attacks vanish, check bass note length and competing transients as well as EQ.
5. Give rhythm, octave, lead and texture guitars distinct roles. Compare balance/pan before carving every track. Independent doubles can supply width; a copied take with delay is a different effect and needs a mono check. Dense distortion may already have little dynamic range, so additional compression needs an audible reason.
6. Keep the lead intelligible during guitars and fills. Ride phrases before forcing a compressor to solve every buried word. Keep gang-vocal consonants coherent without flattening the crowd effect. Compare controlled rasp with a filtered parallel grit layer only if the desired aesthetic calls for it.
7. Shape contrast using a small number of meaningful changes: a fill lift, lower verse texture, a wider backing-vocal entry or a chorus-only effect. Judge the transition from several seconds before the chorus, not from the chorus alone.

| Symptom | First discriminating test | Reject the change when |
|---|---|---|
| Big guitars, small band | Lower guitars briefly; compare snare and bass audibility | Extra drum brightness only increases fatigue |
| Chorus is louder but smaller | Compare matched-level verse-to-chorus transitions | Compression removes the first-hit impact |
| Vocal is clear only in solo | Check the exact masking guitar phrase and vocal ride | Broad presence boost makes every consonant hard |
| Rhythm width disappears in mono | Compare real doubles and any delayed copy separately | Widening causes hollow centre or unstable pitch |

**Vocal tuning:** preserve scoops and purposeful strain in raw punk; use selective pitch repair for exposed melodic parts when needed. Do not pitch-correct screaming/noise as if it were stable monophonic singing. A hard-tuned vocal is an explicit aesthetic variant, not the default meaning of polished pop-punk.

**GarageBand execution:** static balance, pan and discovered controls can carry most of the first pass. Dynamic EQ, arbitrary parallel buses and per-word plugin automation must be checked in the actual adapter; do not describe macro movement as those techniques. Use verified offline renders or a bounded manual action for a required unavailable operation. Follow the current [adapter contract](../garageband.md).

**Acceptance:** lead words survive the densest refrain; snare and guitar attacks remain distinguishable at quiet level; bass supports rather than blankets the riff; stops stay intentional; mono keeps the hook; the final refrain earns its lift without a loudness trick. Mark these as listening checks, not conclusions from peak statistics.
