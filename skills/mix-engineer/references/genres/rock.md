# Rock: band balance, performance and architecture

**Aim:** a convincing band with intentional weight, space and emotional movement. Establish whether the song wants raw/roomy, intimate, aggressive or highly produced treatment. Reference only the relevant qualities; do not force one era's entire sound onto another arrangement.

## Engineer evidence

Andy Wallace describes a quick full-band balance, section-level decisions, then detailed fader rides after the relationships are established. He distinguishes bass compression for tone from rides that control particular notes, and checks balances at contrasting playback levels. [Linkin Park interview, 2014](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-linkin-parks-hunting-party), source `wallace-2014`.

Manny Marroquin describes automating subtle distortion on bass/guitars to help them emerge in selected sections, and using the lyrics to guide musical emphasis. This suggests a possible alternative to always adding volume or brightness. [Waves interview, 2021](https://www.waves.com/waves-grammys-2021-manny-marroquin-post-malone), source `marroquin-2021`.

## Multitrack decisions — our proposed workflow

1. Map the performance: exposed intro, verse, refrain, solo, climax and ending. Identify which guitar mics belong to one take, which are doubles, and where the room or leakage is part of the band's sound. Keep a useful rough balance for comparison.
2. Put up a fast full-band picture, including lead vocal. Decide what carries each section before getting trapped in individual drum or guitar tone. Check the loudest section and the most exposed section early.
3. Treat the drum kit as one perspective. Compare close/overhead/room contributions and same-source polarity where needed. If the snare is small, first distinguish poor combination, short envelope, masking and simple level. A sample is not an automatic remedy.
4. Find a bass–guitar relationship with both present. Bass can supply body under leaner guitars, or guitars may carry more weight in a sparse trio. Keep bass pitch audible through the song, including notes that vanish or jump out. Prefer a local correction when only a few notes misbehave.
5. Shape guitars by role and arrangement. Separate rhythm width from a centred melodic hook or solo. A louder solo can change the apparent drum balance; compare it with the surrounding phrase. Do not add identical saturation to every instrument and expect separation.
6. Work the vocal in the band, including consonants near snare hits and phrases under lead guitar. Preserve expressive rasp. Compare a modest ride with more compression; check whether additional ambience moves the singer too far from the band.
7. Build the arc: an intimate opening must have enough body to stand alone, while a climax needs space to grow. Protect intentional pauses, feedback, reverb tails and final decay. Review the full track after local fixes to avoid a collection of individually impressive but disconnected sections.

| Symptom | First discriminating test | Reject the change when |
|---|---|---|
| Guitar is huge in solo but small in the band | Compare midrange, bass support and competing parts | More low end makes the whole band cloudy |
| Snare seems to change with each chorus | Compare masking and bus gain reduction | EQ brightening fixes one section and hurts another |
| Singer loses emotional emphasis | Compare original phrase dynamics with the processed vocal | Uniform level erases the lyric's shape |
| Bigger room makes drums smaller | Compare direct/room balance and decay | Transients retreat behind constant wash |
| Mix sounds good only loudly | Check quiet-level vocal, backbeat and bass pitch | Detail relies entirely on extra playback level |

**Vocal tuning:** retain stylistic slides, vibrato and roughness unless instructed otherwise. Repair an exposed wrong note with its local harmony in mind; a slow ballad can reveal artifacts a dense chorus conceals. Distorted or doubled vocal treatments should be judged for meaning and intelligibility, not added because the genre is rock.

**GarageBand execution:** translate the desired result into supported edits. Do not promise console-style buses, phase tools, automated distortion or sample-accurate rides from generic Smart Controls. Follow [adapter capabilities](../garageband.md), use a verified render path for unsupported necessities and record the limitation.

**Acceptance:** the band feels connected; the vocal communicates at quiet level; guitars and drums remain distinct in dense moments; bass notes hold together; mono retains weight; sparse sections stay intentionally sparse; the ending decays naturally. Any claim about feel or fatigue requires actual listening.
